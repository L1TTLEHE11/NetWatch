How to choose the best Wi-Fi channel

In apartment buildings dozens of networks share the same few channels, and each one slows the others down. Moving your router to a free channel is often the cheapest speed boost you can get.

1. Look at the air

Open Wi-Fi → Spectrum in NetWatch. Every nearby network is drawn as a hill over the channels it uses; the higher the hill, the stronger the signal. Where hills overlap, networks interfere with each other.

2. Check the recommendation

  • 2.4 GHz: only channels 1, 6 and 11 don’t overlap, so NetWatch picks the least loaded of them.
  • 5 GHz: there are many more channels and much less congestion; NetWatch recommends one of 36–48 or 149–165, which work with every device and don’t require radar detection (DFS).
  • If your current channel is already fine, NetWatch says so — no need to change anything.

3. Change the channel on the router

Open the router’s settings page (its address is on the NetWatch home screen), go to the wireless settings and set the recommended channel instead of “Auto”. In crowded buildings use a 20 MHz channel width on 2.4 GHz — wider channels collide with more neighbours.

4. Check the result

Run a Speed test before and after the change, and look at the signal graph on the home screen. NetWatch keeps every test in History, so the difference is easy to compare.

Tips

  • If your devices support it, prefer 5 GHz: it is faster and less crowded, but reaches less far through walls.
  • Signal weak in some rooms? Walk around with Wi-Fi → Site survey and measure each spot — it shows where you need a second access point.
  • Android allows only about 4 Wi-Fi scans every 2 minutes. If the graph doesn’t refresh, wait a little.
