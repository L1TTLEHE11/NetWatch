How to see who is connected to your Wi-Fi

A neighbour using your Wi-Fi, a forgotten smart plug or an old phone — all of them slow your network down and some are a security risk. Here is how to list every device on your network from an Android phone.

1. Connect to the network you want to check

Your phone must be on the same Wi-Fi as the devices you are looking for. Install NetWatch and allow location access when asked — Android needs it to show the network name.

2. Run the scanner

Open Network → Scanner and tap Scan network. NetWatch checks every address of your subnet in 10–30 seconds. It sends several pings and probes common ports, so it also finds devices that ignore a single ping, such as TVs in power-saving mode.

3. Read the list

  • Type — router, MikroTik, phone, iPhone, Windows PC, printer, IP camera, Android TV, Smart TV, NAS.
  • Name — taken from NetBIOS, mDNS and UPnP, e.g. “Living Room TV” or “OFFICE-PC”.
  • Open ports — what services a device offers (web page, file sharing, remote desktop).

4. Find out what an unknown device is

  • Compare the list with the devices you own: phones, laptops, TV, consoles, smart speakers, cameras, the printer.
  • Switch your own devices off one by one and scan again — whatever disappears was yours.
  • Open the router’s page in a browser (the address is shown on the NetWatch home screen) and look at its list of connected clients or DHCP leases.
  • Have a MikroTik? Connect it in More → MikroTik: the scanner then shows MAC addresses and names from the router.

5. Let NetWatch watch for new devices

The scanner remembers devices for each network. Next time, everything that wasn’t there before is marked NEW, so a stranger stands out immediately.

Found a stranger? Lock them out

  • Change the Wi-Fi password in the router settings — every device will have to reconnect.
  • Use WPA2 or WPA3 security; never “open” or WEP (NetWatch shows the security type of every network in Wi-Fi → Spectrum).
  • Turn off WPS and change the router’s admin password from the default.

Why some devices may be missing

Phones in deep sleep and devices with a strict firewall may not answer at all — scan again after a minute. Android does not let apps read other devices’ MAC addresses, so NetWatch identifies devices by their behaviour, names and ports instead.
